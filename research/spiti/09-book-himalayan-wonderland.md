# "Himalayan Wonderland: Travels in Lahaul and Spiti" (Manohar Singh Gill) — new material

Source: real print book (PDF owned by Mr. X, bookseller). Author was IAS officer posted in the region (1961-era), later Governor/Chief Election Commissioner of India — genuine administrative-history credibility. Read: TOC, "Karding Monastery" ch. (printed 82-97), "The Reason Why" ch. (printed 236-249) + Glossary, "Marriage and Polyandry" ch. (printed 184-195), "Winter Vignettes" opening (196).

**Critical finding: this book is heavily Lahaul-focused, not Spiti.** Several promising-titled chapters turned out to be exclusively about Lahaul:
- **"Karding Monastery"** — entirely Kyelang/Karding village/Dilburi peak, Lahaul's Bhaga valley. Not Spiti.
- **"Marriage and Polyandry"** — explicitly, repeatedly stated as "the general practice in **Lahaul**" throughout (kidnap-marriage "nya," bagthidpa ceremony, pagsgormo chhaang bowls). **Do NOT attribute this chapter's customs to Spiti** — different valley, different practice. Existing research's thin Spiti-polyandry paragraph stands un-supplemented by this book.

## Genuinely new, Spiti-specific findings

1. **Key Monastery gender exclusion** (p.91, comparative aside): Gill writes *"In Spiti, the stricter yellow-hat sect predominates, and I did not see women at Kye monastery or elsewhere."* Real first-hand observation — Key (Gelug/yellow-hat) apparently excludes women/nuns, unlike Lahaul's mixed-gender red-hat monasteries (married lamas, nuns living alongside monks). Single-observer, undated within a 1961-2009 visit span — flag as first-hand-but-single-source, not confirmed institutional policy.

2. **Kaza gully-danda match, dated historical anecdote** (p.241): Punjab CM Sardar Pratap Singh Kairon (who created Lahaul-Spiti as its own district, June 1960) held Tribes Advisory Council meetings alternately in Lahaul/Spiti; "at Kaza, the headquarters of Spiti, Kairon once famously organized a gully-danda match between the ministers and the secretaries" (gully-danda = traditional North Indian street game). Small, genuine, dated, Kaza-specific color not found in any web source.

3. **Real administrative founding history of Lahaul-Spiti district** (p.238-244): administered as part of Kangra district until June 1960, when broken off into its own district (DC at Kyelang, separate SDMs for Lahaul and Spiti) — specifically because of 1958-onward India-China border tension (Chip-Chap river clashes), not routine reorganization. Confirms transfer from Punjab to the newly-formed Himachal Pradesh in 1966. Existing research only has the *current* admin structure (3 sub-divisions, 41 panchayats), not this founding history or its real cause.

4. **Gill's 2008 Chandratal trip, altitude-sickness anecdote** (p.245-247): real recent family visit, acute AMS incident at Chandratal specifically; mentions Shigri-glacier geologists carrying "Coca-30" homeopathic pills for altitude problems. Specific remedy name not in existing logistics research — flag explicitly as anecdotal folk practice, NOT a medical endorsement.

## Not independently useful / already covered / not Spiti
Chandratal's crescent shape, golden brahmani ducks, general isolation — already covered elsewhere. Glossary terms mostly generic-Tibetan or explicitly Lahaul-only (Gotsi, Halda — Halda already correctly flagged elsewhere in this project as Lahaul-only, confirmed again here).

## Unread — flagged for a possible follow-up pass, not done this round
"The Rising of the Dead"/"Tales of the Dead" (p.107-128) and **Appendix I "Major Monasteries in Lahaul and Spiti" (p.255+)** and Appendix III (trekking routes) — ran out of scoped budget. Appendix I in particular is likely to have a concise real Spiti-specific monastery list and is the highest-value next read if this extraction continues.

**Bottom line:** this book skews Lahaul-heavy — genuine new Spiti material was thin (4 items above), not the deep vein hoped for. The unread appendices are the most promising remaining section.
