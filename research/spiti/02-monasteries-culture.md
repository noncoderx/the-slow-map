# Spiti Valley — Monasteries & Buddhist Culture (research pass, Aug 2026)

## 1. Key Monastery (Ki Gompa)
- Founding: traditionally 11th century by Dromtön (disciple of Atisha) — but current buildings/institution reorganized as Gelug establishment after Mongol raids in 17th century (5th Dalai Lama era). Treat "11th-c founding" as traditional attribution, not archaeological date.
- Sect: Gelug — one of 3 Gelugpa monasteries in Spiti (with Tabo, Dhankar)
- Altitude: ~4,116-4,166m, overlooking Kaza
- Scale: largest monastery in Spiti Valley, tiered/fort-like stack on conical hill
- Monks: ~100 recorded 1855; ~250-300 today (approximate, travel sources not census)
- 1,000-yr anniversary reportedly held 2000 w/ Dalai Lama present (single-source, not cross-verified)
- Contents: murals, thangkas, manuscripts, stucco images, weapons collection, ritual instruments
- Festival: annual Chaam/Cham masked dance ("Gustor") — timing inconsistently reported (summer June/July per some; Tibetan lunar calendar shifts yearly regardless) — don't treat month as fixed

## 2. Tabo Monastery
- Founded 996 CE by translator-lama Rinchen Zangpo, on behalf of Yeshe-Ö (king of Guge)
- Widely described as one of oldest continuously operating Buddhist monastic enclaves in India/Himalayas — consistent across nearly every source
- Nickname: "Ajanta of the Himalayas" (near-universal usage, refers to murals)
- Complex: 9 temples, several chortens (one source: 23), cave shrines cut into cliff, monk/nun quarters — "largest monastic complex in Spiti" per district tourism page
- Sect: Gelug
- UNESCO tentative list: widely repeated across secondary/tourism sources but NOT independently confirmed against primary whc.unesco.org database this pass — flag as widely-reported-not-primary-verified
- Protected as national monument by ASI
- 1996: Dalai Lama reportedly conducted Kalachakra initiation coinciding with millennium celebrations (one source)

## 3. Dhankar Monastery
- Location: cliff/spur ~1,000ft high over Spiti-Pin confluence, ~32km from Kaza near Shichling, ~3,870-3,894m
- Founding: DISPUTED across sources — 7th/11th/12th century all cited, no consensus. Report as disputed, not resolved.
- Historical role: former capital of Spiti Kingdom, seat of hereditary local rulers ("Nono") who held de facto local power (distant king rarely visited). One source: functioned as capital "during 17th century" while monastery/fort structure older — different periods, not resolved.
- Sect: Gelugpa, reportedly since 1450s (chronology inconsistency w/ founding-date dispute, flagged not resolved)
- Monks: ~150 lamas currently (one source)
- **Structural/erosion — well documented:** cliff base highly vulnerable to wind/rain erosion, progressive crumbling of older (12th-c-attributed) masonry. **2006: World Monuments Fund listed Dhankar among 100 Most Endangered Sites** — single most concrete/citable fact here. 1975 earthquake severely damaged complex. New Dhankar Monastery built nearby for continuing monastic life while old structure's conservation managed separately.

## 4. Kungri Monastery (Pin Valley)
- Location: Pin Valley, ~10km from Attargo, 3km from Gulling
- Founding: local tradition ~1330 CE (early 14th c.)
- Spiti's 2nd-oldest monastery; ONLY Nyingma-school monastery in Spiti (oldest of 4 main Tibetan Buddhist traditions, assoc. w/ Guru Padmasambhava/Guru Rinpoche, 8th c.)
- Architecture: 3 rectangular blocks, all facing east
- Art: wall paintings/manuscripts described by one source as "up to 1,000 years old" — predates 1330 founding date, likely imprecise language not verified dating — flagged inconsistency
- Festival — devil/sword dance: annual, typically July. Sword-dance sequence led specifically by "buzhens"(Buchen) lay-ritualists from Mud village (Nyingma ritual tradition, distinct from monk-performed Cham at Gelug monasteries). Sword-swallowing claims NOT verified — sources describe dramatic sword sequences/devil dances, not swallowing.

## 5. Tangyud Monastery (Komic)
- Location: near Komic, ~4,500m, one of highest-altitude monasteries in region. One source: originally near Hikkim, relocated after 1975 earthquake.
- Founding: early 14th century (district govt source)
- Sect: sources mostly say Sakya (more specific/consistent sourcing) — treat as Sakya, not the Gelugpa/"Drangtse" naming confusion found in one secondary source
- Lamas reportedly completed revision of 87-volume Tang-rGyud Tantra treatises; regarded as proficient in tantric practice (district tourism source)

## 6. Lhalung Monastery ("Golden Temple"/Serkhang)
- Founded by Rinchen Zangpo, late 10th c. — among earliest Spiti monasteries, contemporaneous/just before Tabo. Tradition credits him with founding 108 western-Himalaya monasteries.
- Inner shrine "Serkhang" = "Golden Temple" — named for gilded frescoes/artwork depicting 50+ deities, not a golden spire
- Foundation legend: built "overnight by the gods" after Rinchen Zangpo planted a willow tree, promising a temple if it survived a year — tree still said to stand outside gompa
- Function: historic Choshor (seat of religious learning/doctrinal debate), not just village temple
- Possible identity overlap: a separate result mentions "Sherkhang Monastery at Lalung" possibly older than Tabo — very likely SAME site under alternate transliteration (Lalung=Lhalung, Sherkhang≈Serkhang), not a 6th distinct monastery — don't double-count

## 7. Other named gompas — lower documentation depth
- Kibber: primarily described as high-altitude village (14,200ft), not a distinct major gompa
- Poh, Demul, Kanamo: no specific monastery details found — flag unconfirmed, don't fabricate
- Guru Ghantal Monastery, Tayul Monastery: real and well-documented but located in LAHAUL not Spiti (Tayul near Keylong) — explicitly rule out of Spiti chapter despite appearing on generic "Lahaul-Spiti monastery" listicles

## 8. Local Buddhist Culture — Sects & Daily Life
**Sect map (verified):** Gelug — Key, Tabo, Dhankar ("one of three Gelugpa establishments in Spiti" per one source). Nyingma — Kungri only. Sakya — Tangyud.

- Buddhism deeply interwoven with daily village life (not confined to worship) — monastic performing arts, thangka painting, carpet weaving, agricultural rituals, life-cycle rites — sourcing here general/summary-level not granular
- Social structure: semi-feudal, headed locally by hereditary "Nono" chieftain lineage (distant king rarely visited in person). Nonos held rights to cultivate govt lands, obligated to maintain Dhankar fort. Nono-monastery land-tenure relationship not well covered — flagged, not filled with invented detail.
- Language: Spiti Bhoti, Tibetic/Sino-Tibetan, ~74% lexically similar to Lahuli/Stod Bhoti (Lahaul). Classified endangered — spoken by adults, declining transmission to younger generation, not formally taught in schools.
- Traditional dress: women wear "Sulma" (long voluminous gown, local wool — yak/sheep/goat). Festival jewelry: turquoise-studded Perak/Berag (head ornaments), pearl Uldik, silver Docha, Dhikra (brooch), Khinyur, Pitsup. Unmarried women don't wear Berag/Perak (married-only). Historically only higher-status "Khangchen" households could afford full genuine-turquoise Berag sets vs lower-status "Khang Chung" — real sourced example of jewelry encoding social rank.

|---|---|---|---|
| Losar (Tibetan New Year) | Across Spiti | **Distinctively earlier than rest of Tibetan Buddhist world** — mid-Nov to early-Dec in Lahaul-Spiti (local lunar-calendar variant) vs standard Tibetan Losar late-Jan-March elsewhere. Most-repeated specific claim, consistent across sources. | Standard household/new-year rites — granular Spiti-specific ritual detail not found beyond date divergence |
| Chaam/Cham (Key Monastery) | Key Monastery | Conflicting month — summer(Jun/Jul) vs "Gustor" name associated elsewhere (Ladakh specifically) w/ autumn/winter dates. Exact Key date NOT pinned down — flag explicitly, moves yearly w/ lunar calendar regardless | Monks in ornate robes/carved deity masks, slow ritualized circular dances to horns/drums/cymbals |
| Devil Dance/Sword Dance (Kungri) | Kungri Monastery, Pin Valley | Annually, typically July | Cham masked dances + sword-dance sequence by Buchen lay-ritualists from Mud village — distinct from monk-performed Cham at Gelug sites |
| Ladarcha Fair | Kaza (originally near Chicham/Kibber) | Currently 3rd week August | Originated as real trans-Himalayan barter trade fair (Spiti-Ladakh-Tibet-Kinnaur-Rampur Bushahr), historically held every July. Ceased after 1962 Sino-Indian war cut trans-border trade; revived by HP govt in 1980s as state cultural festival, relocated to Kaza. Now cultural festival + street market, not the original barter event — real documented before/after shift. |
| Halda | **LAHAUL ONLY** (Gahar, Chandra, Pattan valleys) — sources EXPLICITLY confirm NOT a Spiti festival despite appearing on combined "Lahaul-Spiti" lists | Month-long from January (peak 2nd-3rd week) | Cedar-branch torch per male family member, lit, placed in homes; blessings for harvest, warding evil spirits. Included specifically to CORRECT common Lahaul/Spiti conflation — don't describe as Spiti custom. |
| Shivratri | Region-wide incl. Lahaul-Spiti | Standard Hindu calendar | Nominally Hindu, celebrated by Buddhist locals too — cited as religious syncretism example, detail thin/general |
