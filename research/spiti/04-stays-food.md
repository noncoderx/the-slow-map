# Spiti Valley — Stays & Food, village by village (research pass, Aug 2026)

## Homestay Culture Overview — Spiti Ecosphere Network
**Spiti Ecosphere** — real, verifiable community-tourism org, founded 2002 (Ishita Khanna + associates), social enterprise. Runs documented homestay program across **6 villages: Langza, Komic, Demul, Lhalung(Lalung), Dhankar, Mikkim** — all ~4,000m avg altitude, within/near Kibber Wildlife Sanctuary. Up to 3 homestays/village, 1 guest bedroom each for 1-2 people — deliberately capped, not scaled into hotels. Won Virgin Holidays Responsible Tourism Award 2010. (spitiecosphere.com, Wikipedia)

Kibber documented separately, runs more as independent larger/longer-established village-homestay economy (not formally in the Ecosphere 6-village list).

General pricing across these villages: **₹700-1,800/person/night**, usually incl. breakfast+dinner (home-cooked). Rooms typically basic — mud-brick, shared bathrooms mostly, some newer homestays now offer attached Western bathrooms as differentiator.

## Village-by-Village Stays

**Kibber (4,270m)** — large established village in Kibber Wildlife Sanctuary, snow-leopard-tracking base. Named stays: Tashi Homestay, Deshek Homestay (NoM Deshek), Norling Homestay (confirmed winter-open). ~₹800+/night meal-inclusive; Airbnb-style from ~₹1,834/night. Winter: inhabited year-round, multiple homestays stay open — unlike higher seasonal hamlets.

**Langza (4,400m) — "Fossil Village"** — homestays primary accommodation. Named: Lara's Homestay (5-6 rooms, some deluxe w/ attached Western bathroom). ₹1,200-1,800/person/night w/ meals. Winter: Tanzi Homestay confirmed operating; most families live year-round. **Fossil collection now legally restricted by HP govt** — real, currently-enforced rule.

**Komic (4,587m)** — Ecosphere network, basic mud-brick/shared bathroom, no phone signal, limited power. ₹800-1,500/person w/ meals (bundled w/ Langza/Hikkim price band). Home to Tangyud Monastery. Road access **May-Oct only** — treat as summer-only-access village; no winter-operating homestay confirmed (gap).

**Demul (4,340m) — "the homestay hub"** — Ecosphere network, specifically a **model sustainable-tourism village**: rotational homestay system, roughly half households host one year/other half next year, income distributed evenly rather than concentrated near the road. Known for Arak (local barley brew) + folk performances. Solar power/water conservation documented. No specific named/priced homestay surfaced beyond network structure — thin on names. Winter: "Demul in Winter" content exists implying some winter interest/access but no specific homestay confirmed open — unverified, not assumed.

**Lhalung/Lalung (~4,020m)** — Ecosphere network, known for local artists/musicians/cultural performances. "Land of the Gods" framing (Tripoto, single-source-type). No specific homestay names/prices surfaced — genuinely thin, flagged not invented.

**Dhankar (3,894m)** — **homestays only, no hotels** (stated explicitly in sources). A few homestays near the monastery, basic bed+food+toilet. Price consistent with region-wide ₹700-1,500/person band, no Dhankar-specific price confirmed. Dhankar Lake trek is main overnight draw (vs. day-trip).

**Mud (Mudh), Pin Valley (3,770m)** — last village before Pin Valley NP/border zone. Real named stays: **Mudh Homestay** (opened 2004, one yr after motorable road reached — first homestay in Pin Valley), **Tara Homestay and Guesthouse** (long-running family op, traditional rooms, clean bathrooms), **Ibex Homestay** (locally run, basic tier). Trailhead for Pin-Parvati (to Barshaini) and Bhaba Pass (to Kaphnoo) treks — serves trekkers as much as sightseers. Noted for exceptionally dark skies (near-zero light pollution).

**Kungri** — NOT confirmed as independent stay village; reads as monastery day-trip site from Mud (famous Chham mask-dance festival, typically July), not a separate overnight base with its own homestay network. No Kungri-specific accommodation found — don't invent.

## Kaza — Hub Town (only real hotel/guesthouse market in Spiti)
- Scale: ~16-21+ bookable hotel/guesthouse properties depending on platform, budget to comfort tier. Budget from ₹520-680/night.
- Budget: numerous small guesthouses (Hotel Zangchuk, Kunga Hotel and Restaurant cited as popular)
- Mid/comfort tier: **Hotel Deyzor** (widely cited best-loved, 10 rooms, electric blankets, cable TV, hot water, 24/7 solar+generator power, in-house restaurant Indian/Tibetan/European). **Sakya Abode** (near Kaza monastery, 12 rooms, traditional architecture; winter operation INCONSISTENT year-to-year — "contact to confirm" is the honest answer, not fixed yes/no). **Grand Dewachen** (~12,000ft, traditional+modern, rooftop restaurant valley views, more upmarket). **Zostel Spiti** (hostel/dorm option, backpacker-social niche, distinct from both guesthouses and comfort hotels).
- Café Deyzor (attached to Hotel Deyzor) separately called out for momos.

## Tabo — Monastery Village
- **Tabo Monastery Guest House** (aka "Millennium Monastery Guest House") — real, documented, attached to monastery itself, stay-with-monks at nominal cost, basic accommodation + basic traditional meals. Real "monastery stay" option, not folklore.
- Independent stays: **Tabo Homestay** (short walk from monastery, heaters, free Wi-Fi — modest but heated, notable at this altitude), **The Spiti Villa** (more upscale, traditional-meets-modern, apple-orchard views).
- Tabo's guesthouse tradition of hosting pilgrims/travelers appears long-standing, not a recent tourism add-on.

## Seasonal Closures
- **Year-round-population villages → some homestays stay open winter:** Kibber (Norling Homestay), Kaza (a few near bus stand), Langza (Tanzi Homestay)
- **Road-cut villages → effectively summer-only (~May-Oct):** Komic explicitly confirmed. Other high hamlets likely follow by altitude/exposure logic, but no explicit per-village winter-access confirmation found for Demul, Lhalung, Dhankar, Mud — **disclosed gap, don't assume open or closed without a source**.
- Winter tourism real/growing: snow-leopard tracking (Kibber/Pin Valley) is biggest draw, plus stargazing over Kaza, frozen-waterfall walks near Key/Kibber, cultural homestay experiences (cooking, weaving). Shimla-Kaza road stays main winter access; internal village roads may close after heavy snowfall even when main route holds.
- Even Kaza hotel-tier winter operation not guaranteed at property level (Sakya Abode example).

## Food & Cuisine
**Core real dishes (Tibetan/Ladakhi-influenced Spitian staples):**
- Thukpa — Tibetan noodle soup, hand-pulled flat wheat noodles, meat/veg broth
- Thentuk — thicker relative, flat hand-torn noodle pieces, denser vegetable-forward soup
- Momos — steamed/fried dumplings, everywhere
- Chhurpi — local cheese (yak/female-yak milk), tangy/firm, standalone snack or in soups/stir-fries
- Tsampa — roasted barley flour, genuine dietary staple, mixed into dishes or eaten w/ butter tea
- Barley soup + barley/buckwheat dishes — crops actually suited to cold-desert climate, historic diet base before green peas as cash crop
- Butter Tea (Po Cha) — defining local drink: strong black tea churned (100+ strokes traditionally) w/ yak butter+salt into thick savory soup-like drink. Hospitality staple, offered as welcome drink. Salty/savory not sweet — surprises first-timers.

Homestays = more authentic source (hosts cook traditional dishes as part of stay). Kaza café scene blends genuine local dishes w/ tourist fare (pizza, pancakes, waffles, hot chocolate) for backpacker crowd.

**Named cafés/restaurants (recur across multiple independent sources):**
- **Sol Café** — run by Spiti Ecosphere itself, opened 2012, Kaza main market, converted mud house, blends Spitian+international (pizza/pancakes/chocolate sandwiches/waffles). Most-cited café in Kaza.
- **Yak Café** — Continental/Tibetan/Chinese, repeatedly called one of most renowned.
- **Himalayan Café** — most popular all-purpose spot, locals+tourists.
- **Café Deyzor** (Hotel Deyzor) — best momos in town.
- **Piti Café** — Spitian pizza, jhol momos (wet/soupy momo style), hot chocolate.

## Organic Farming / Green Peas & Barley
- Green pea cultivation began 1983 (local family, experimental) — became dominant cash crop over next 2 decades alongside apples, gradually displacing subsistence crops (barley, buckwheat, pulses).
- Academic source (Science Advances, biocultural vulnerability of traditional crops in Trans-Himalaya) confirms real tension: **traditional crops (barley, black pea/kala matar) actually outperform introduced green pea in survival/reproduction trials** — the shift to green peas is an ECONOMIC choice, not agronomic improvement. Real citable nuance, not a simple "peas are great" narrative.
- Spiti Ecosphere runs farming-tourism/training programs — visitors learn sweet pea, kala matar (black pea), sattu (barley), tsirku (wild seabuckthorn) farming — direct verified link between organic/traditional-crop story and local food culture: barley + seabuckthorn are actively farmed, base ingredients behind tsampa, barley soup, seabuckthorn juice sold to tourists valley-wide.
- No named formal "organic cooperative" brand found — Ecosphere is the closest verified organizing body; presented as Ecosphere's agricultural-training program, not invented as a co-op brand.
