# Spiti Valley — Logistics & Practical Field Guide (research pass, Aug 2026)

**Caveat:** Spiti's permit rules have flip-flopped historically (imposed 2017, relaxed/reinstated cycles reported through 2021). Sources agree on the current (2026) picture below, but re-verify if this guide is republished 6-12mo+ out.

## 1. Permits (ILP / PAP)

**Indian nationals: NO permit required.** Valid photo ID (Aadhaar/DL/passport) shown on request at checkpoints (Jangi, Sumdo) — that's a security register, not a permit check (common traveler confusion, flagged by multiple blogs).

**Foreign nationals: Protected Area Permit (PAP) required** for restricted stretch near Tibet border (Kaza, Tabo, Dhankar, Sumdo zones).
- Offline only — no online application as of early 2026
- Apply at: SDM Reckong Peo (most-used, Shimla-Kinnaur route), SDM Pooh (Kinnaur), ADC office Kaza, or DM Shimla
- "Min 2 foreign travelers" nominally enforced, but solo travelers report success without an agent at Reckong Peo/Pooh/Kaza
- Documents: passport+valid Indian visa, photos, photocopies (triplicate), itinerary, Form A, ideally travel-agency letter
- Validity: ~10 days + 7-day extension option
- Cost: ~₹250-400 (varies by office, third-party estimates only — no official fee schedule found)

**Unresolved:** exact historical ILP timeline disputed across sources; no official govt fee source found — verify directly with Kaza ADC or Reckong Peo SDM before publishing an exact number.

## 2. Roads

**Route A: Manali–Kaza** (via Atal Tunnel + Gramphu–Batal–Kunzum Pass)
- Atal Tunnel (opened Oct 2020, 9.02km, 24/7 year-round) bypasses Rohtang Pass bottleneck
- Real bottleneck: Kunzum Pass (~4,590m) + rough Gramphu–Batal stretch
- Season: closes mid-late Oct, reopens late May-early June (tunnel access shaved ~2-3wks off historical opening)
- 2026: open by late May, confirmed through mid-July
- Shorter (~12-14hr driving) but steep 1-2 day altitude jump — named as the route causing AMS in "vast majority" of affected travelers

**Route B: Shimla–Kaza** (via Kinnaur, NH-05)
- Season: April-November, only viable winter entry (experienced 4x4 driver needed for black ice)
- Better surface but monsoon landslide risk: Maling Nala, Nigulsari, Wangtu-Sumdo (worst July-Aug)
- NH-5 sustained heavy 2025-monsoon damage near Nigulsari/Pooh-Kaurik, repairs ongoing through 2026 — expect single-lane stretches
- Example: Rohli-Kaddu nullah (SKTT road) suspended until Jul 31 2026 for landslide restoration — short-notice closures are a recurring live feature
- Longer (~20+hrs over several days) but gentler "stair-step" altitude gain — recommended for acclimatization

**Practical routing:** enter via Shimla-Kinnaur (gradual), exit via Manali-Kunzum (or reverse) — classic loop, covers both landscapes + best acclimatization. Build 2-3 buffer days into monsoon-season (Jul-Sep) itineraries for landslide delays. Confirm road status day-before AND morning-of — conditions change fast, don't trust a check >1-2 days old.

## 3. Altitude & Acclimatization (Spiti-specific)

- Elevation range: ~3,600-6,600m across ~1,400 sq km
- Kaza: ~3,650-3,800m | Komic: ~4,587m (highest motorable village, contested) | Kibber: ~4,270m | Hikkim: ~4,440m | Chandratal: 4,300m+ (noticeably thinner than Kaza)

**Spiti-specific guidance:**
- Biggest lever = route choice: Shimla-Kinnaur-Kaza = gradual stair-step gain over days; Manali-Kunzum = steep 1-2 day jump over 14,900ft+ pass — named cause of most AMS cases
- Staged stops: 1 night Reckong Peo/Kalpa (~2,960m) → 1 night Tabo (~3,280m) → Kaza — travelers following this are typically well-acclimatized by higher villages
- Avoid first night at Komic/Kibber/Chandratal, especially arriving from Manali side
- Chandratal only after 2-3 comfortable nights already at/above ~3,600m
- Hydration: 3-4L/day (arid high-desert compounds AMS dehydration risk beyond standard advice)
- No alcohol/smoking first 2-3 days
- Rest on arrival at any new higher stop before exploring
- Descent warning signs: symptoms worsening (not improving) after 24hrs, confusion, difficulty walking, breathlessness at rest

## 4. Connectivity

- Best coverage: **Kaza** — BSNL AND Jio 4G both work with reasonably stable data (2026 update: not BSNL-only anymore)
- BSNL most broadly reliable across the valley generally (Kaza, Tabo, most major villages)
- Airtel thin — cities + thinning bit of Kinnaur, not deep into Spiti
- Total dead zone: Gramphu→Chatru→Chota Dhara→Batal→Chandratal→Kunzum Pass — ~60km zero bars, longest dead stretch in HP
- 2025-26 infra news: DoT Intra Circle Roaming (ICR) launched in Kullu/Mandi districts (adjacent, NOT confirmed in Lahaul-Spiti itself — flag as nearby-not-confirmed); BSNL 4G→5G upgrade plan by mid-2026 (statewide, not Spiti-specific confirmed)
- Practical: BSNL postpaid as primary bet, Jio-in-Kaza as bonus not guarantee, download offline maps for the dead stretches

## 5. Money & Fuel

**ATMs:** Kaza has the only ones — 2 SBI ATMs, both explicitly unreliable (frequent cash-outs peak season, multi-day network downtime). Carry ₹5,000-8,000 cash/person/3days, withdrawn before entering valley (Shimla/Manali/Reckong Peo).

**Fuel:** IndianOil pump in Kaza = only one inside Spiti proper, ~9AM-5PM, has run dry in peak season. Nearest alternatives outside valley: Tandi (Lahaul, ~105km from Manali) or Reckong Peo. Fill full at Tandi/Reckong Peo before entering, top up at Kaza, don't count on fuel deeper in valley (Losar/Kibber/Langza/Komic have none).

## 6. Best Time to Visit (by traveler type)

| Type | Window | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Standard/road-open season | Late May/June – mid-Oct | Both routes driveable; peak crowds Jul-Aug coincide w/ Kinnaur landslide risk (Spiti itself rain-shadowed, stays dry) |
| Photography/scenic clarity | Late Sept – early Oct | Sweet spot: clear skies, mild days (14-22°C), cold nights, minimal rain, autumn gold/orange, aqua-green rivers, fewer tourists, apple harvest near Tabo/Hurling/Kaza. 2nd half of Oct increasingly unpredictable (snow risk) |
| Snow leopard | Dec-Mar, strongest late Dec/early Jan | Leopards descend to lower elevations for prey; best around Kibber/Chicham; Pin Valley cited as one of India's best snow-leopard sites |
| Winter "whiteout" Spiti (niche, growing) | Late Jan – mid-Feb | Peak snow cover, -25 to -30°C; only Shimla-Kinnaur route open, needs experienced driver for black ice; expect frozen pipes/no running water/limited electricity in homestays — real hardship tourism |
| Shoulder (Apr-May) | Before full road opening | Scenic, low-crowd, access-limited (Manali route likely still closed) — effectively Shimla-Kinnaur only, volatile conditions |
