# "One Man Goes On A Bus: Spiti and Ladakh by public transport" — new material (pp.61-120)

Source: real print travel memoir (PDF owned by Mr. X, bookseller). Covers the Kinnaur-approach → Spiti → exit-via-Kunzum arc (pp.61-120); remaining ~35pp are Manali R&R then Ladakh, out of scope. Page numbers are PDF-internal, approximate, not printed page numbers.

**Verifiability note:** everything here is one traveler's first-hand, dated (year not stated in pages read) account — not independently fact-checked. Logistics (bridges, bus routes, lodge operators) can change year to year; treat accordingly.

## Literary connection (genuinely new)
- **Kipling's "Kim" is partly set in Spiti** — author states this explicitly twice (Tabo ~p.89, Chandratal ~p.113), calling it "the only piece of literature which features Spiti." That framing is the author's own claim — verify independently before treating as settled, but the Kim-Spiti connection itself is real/checkable (Teshoo Lama's journey with Kim passes through Spiti). Real differentiator — no other Spiti site runs this angle.
- **Harish Kapadia wrote a dedicated Spiti Valley book** — seen in Tabo monastery guesthouse library (~p.90). Real mountaineering author. Good "further reading" citation.

## Practical/logistics detail (real, ground-level, un-web-documented)
- **Bridge-crossing shuttle near Kaza→Kibber**: no direct bus all the way — HRTC buses stop at a bridge, passengers walk across, jeeps wait on the far side (~p.99-100). A taxi driver tried falsely claiming "bridge unsafe" to extort a fare, backed down when called out.
- **No direct transport Dhankar→Kaza** — author got a lift from a stranger's private car, no bus/jeep option existed (~p.91-93). Hitchhiking is a real fallback on this stretch.
- **Khab confluence** (Sutlej+Spiti rivers, the "official" Kinnaur→Spiti transition point): black mineral-streak canyon walls, author compares to Grand Canyon "Indian writing" markings (~p.78). Good scene-setting detail.
- **Malling Nala / "Pagal Nala"** (Kinnaur side): a washed-out bridge + 2km road collapse, travelers walked across while a pulley-cable hauled luggage separately (~p.74-75). Flag as "was true at time of writing," infrastructure-dependent, may be resolved now.
- Kaza-Manali road independently confirmed "legendary for bad condition" — matches existing research, not new but a real independent confirmation.

## Named places not in existing research
- **Unnamed gorge/canyon near Kibber** — swift river, towering cliffs, pebbly bottom, reachable on foot from Kibber village (~p.102-103). NOT the (bridged) Chicham gorge — a separate feature. No official name given in the book.
- **Informal "valley of flowers" near Kibber** — off-trail, found via a dried streambed, wildflowers/rhododendrons/orchids (~p.104-106). Undocumented elsewhere. Report only as "informal wildflower meadows exist off marked trails near Kibber," not as a named attraction.
- **Norbu Lodge, Kibber** — real named homestay/lodge, NOT in the Ecosphere-network list (Tashi/Deshek/Norling) already documented. Traveler-reported memorable stay — flag as unverified-current-operation.
- **A tarpaulin-tent "hotel" directly on the Chandratal lakeshore**, serving tea/instant noodles (~p.113-114). **Real tension with existing research:** the logistics file states lakeshore camping is banned, zones sit 2-3km away. Either the ban postdates this account, enforcement is inconsistent, or the operator has informal/grandfathered permission. Disclose both, don't resolve.
- **Earth-pillar/hoodoo rock formations** en route to Kunzum in the Spiti basin, "an army of soldiers turned to stone" (~p.112) — real geological feature (likely the dried-mud-pillar phenomenon known elsewhere in the Sutlej-Spiti corridor), not currently documented for the Kaza-Kunzum stretch specifically.

## Additional color
- Govt buses double as postal/newspaper delivery on the approach road (Reckong Peo-Nako stretch, Kinnaur side) — mail tossed out at each village, met by cheering villagers (~p.76). Kinnaur-side confirmed; plausibly continues into Spiti on the same HRTC system, not confirmed Spiti-specific.
- **Tabo's bus stand recently modernized** — new stand, shops, electricity, solar lamps, new phone exchange, visible change from author's earlier visit (~p.88). No dates given for either visit.
- **Dhankar etymology, literal**: "Dhang/dang = cliff, kar/khar = fort" → "fort on a cliff" (~p.94). Existing research has the history but not this word-by-word breakdown.
- **Dalai Lama's stated retirement plan is to reside at Tabo Monastery itself** (~p.89) — stronger/more specific than existing research (which only has "attended millennium celebration, one source"). **Flag clearly as this book's claim — needs independent verification before treating as settled fact.** If true, a major differentiating fact.

## Explicitly NOT new (matches existing research closely)
Tabo founding/996CE/Guge/Ajanta-nickname/9-temples; Dhankar cliff-perch/2006 WMF listing/Nono history; Kunzum chorten circumambulation ritual; Chandratal altitude/moon-lake naming; "cold desert, rain-shadow" general framing.
