The Valley
"The middle land"
Between Tibet and everything else.
"Spiti" translates to "the middle land" — the cold-desert corridor between Tibet and the rest of India, often called Little Tibet for its terrain and Buddhist culture.

Geography

Trans-Himalayan region, NE Himachal Pradesh, bordered by Tibet (east), Ladakh (north), Lahaul (west), Kullu (south), Kinnaur (southeast). Valley floor averages ~3,800m; villages and monasteries range from Tabo (~3,050m) to Tashigang (~4,650m). The Spiti River — the Sutlej's largest right-bank tributary — carves the valley, joining the Sutlej at Khab in Kinnaur.

Why it looks like this

A cold high-altitude desert — the Himalayan range blocks nearly all monsoon moisture, so rainfall is essentially absent. Only barley, peas and potatoes survive the climate. Compare Kinnaur next door: monsoon winds reach deeper into that valley, so it stays green and forested while Spiti stays bare, stratified, and multicolored.

Every Village
Not just Kaza. The whole valley, village by village.
Verified against Census 2011, Wikipedia, and district government sources where possible. Faded cards mean the data was too thin to publish with confidence — disclosed, not invented.

Kaza

3,650m · Pop. 3,231 · Hub

Sub-divisional HQ and largest township — split into Kaza Khas (old town, palace ruins) and Kaza Soma (new town, govt offices, hotels).

Kibber

4,270m · Pop. 366 · 19km

Long claimed "highest motorable village in the world" — a title now contested by Komic. Gateway to the 1,400 km² Kibber Wildlife Sanctuary.

Chicham

4,145m · ~85 homes · 22km

Home to Asia's highest suspension bridge (2017, 15 years to build) — before it, villagers crossed the gorge by rope-and-pulley trolley.

Langza

4,420m · Pop. 136 · 16km

The "fossil village" — marine ammonites weather out of rock that was once the floor of the Tethys Sea. A giant Buddha statue overlooks it.

Komic

~4,587m · Pop. 84 · 19km

Marketed via its own PWD signboard as "highest village in the world connected by motorable road." Home to Tangyud Monastery.

Hikkim

4,400m · Pop. ~161 · 18km

World's highest post office, operating since 1983. Also once held India's highest polling booth.

Tashigang

4,650m · ~6 families

Often cited as the highest inhabited village in Spiti. World's highest polling station. Largely snow-cut in winter.

Demul

~4,320m · 53 households · 32km

Runs a rotational community-homestay system — roughly half the households host one year, the other half the next, so income spreads evenly.

Dhankar

3,894m · Pop. 301

Former capital of the Spiti kingdom. Its 1,000-year-old monastery, on a cliff over the Spiti-Pin confluence, was named among the world's 100 Most Endangered Sites in 2006.

Tabo

3,050m

Home to the "Ajanta of the Himalayas" — a monastery founded 996 CE, one of the oldest continuously operating in the Himalayas.

Rangrik

3,780m · Pop. ~900 · 6km

Spiti's largest village by both area and population — unusually green for the region.

Losar

~4,084m · Pop. ~242

First inhabited village after crossing Kunzum La from Manali — also the last Indian village toward the border on this route.

Lhalung

3,658m

Home to the "Golden Temple" (Serkhang) — founded late 10th century by translator-lama Rinchen Zangpo, gilded frescoes of 50+ deities inside.

Gue (Giu)

~3,200m · Pop. 223

Site of a naturally mummified Buddhist monk, Sangha Tenzin, radiocarbon-dated to ~1475 AD — discovered after a 1975 earthquake.

Mud (Mudh)

3,810m · Pop. 213

Last major village in Pin Valley — base camp for the Pin-Parvati and Pin-Bhaba pass treks, and Pin Valley National Park.

Kungri

Pin Valley

Spiti's second-oldest monastery (~1330 CE) and the only one following the Nyingma school — annual devil-dance/sword-dance festival in July.

Mane Yogma & Kogma

3,650m

Twin villages, split by an old flash flood. Mane Yogma is the last Spiti village before the Manirang Pass trail into Kinnaur.

Lingti

Pop. 48, 11 families

One of the smallest recorded villages in Spiti by population. Also gives its name to the valley's one real named waterfall.

Monasteries & Living Culture
Three sects, six monasteries
One disputed founding date, every time.
Gelug at Key, Tabo and Dhankar. Nyingma — the oldest Tibetan Buddhist school — only at Kungri. Sakya at Tangyud.

Key Monastery

Largest in Spiti, ~4,166m, traditionally founded 11th century though reorganized as Gelug after 17th-century Mongol raids. ~250-300 monks in residence.

Its Chaam masked-dance festival date shifts yearly with the Tibetan lunar calendar — no fixed month, despite what some listings claim.

Tabo Monastery

Founded 996 CE by Rinchen Zangpo — one of the oldest continuously operating Buddhist monasteries in the Himalayas. Nine temples, 23 chortens, cave shrines.

Widely claimed as UNESCO tentative-list — repeated everywhere, but not independently confirmed against the primary UNESCO database here.

Dhankar Monastery

Cliff-perched former capital of the Spiti kingdom. Founding century genuinely disputed — 7th, 11th and 12th century all cited, no consensus.

Named among the World Monuments Fund's 100 Most Endangered Sites in 2006 — real, dated, the single hardest fact here.

Kungri Monastery

~1330 CE, Spiti's second-oldest. The valley's only Nyingma monastery. July devil-dance led by Buchen lay-ritualists from Mud village, not monks.

Tangyud Monastery

Near Komic, ~4,500m — one of the highest-altitude functioning monasteries in India. Sakya sect. Relocated after the 1975 earthquake.

Legend: its Mahakala statue "refused to move" during a drought-era relocation attempt — the monks stayed, and the site remains at Komic today.

Lhalung Monastery

Late 10th century, among the earliest in Spiti. Its Serkhang inner shrine — the "Golden Temple" — is named for gilded frescoes, not a golden spire.

Losar (Tibetan New Year) in Spiti falls in mid-November to early December — two to three months earlier than the standard Tibetan Buddhist calendar (Jan-March). The Halda festival that appears on nearly every "Lahaul-Spiti festivals" listicle is actually Lahaul-only — Spiti doesn't celebrate it. Every generic list we checked conflates the two districts.

Getting There
The part every guide gets vague on purpose.
Permit rules and road status genuinely change year to year — everything below is disclosed as a snapshot, not gospel.
Permits

None for Indians

Foreign nationals need a Protected Area Permit (offline only, ~10-day validity) for the Kaza/Tabo/Dhankar interior circuit — apply at Reckong Peo, Pooh, or Kaza. Chandratal alone only needs a lighter ₹50 "Beyond Rohtang" permit.

Route in

Two ways, one safer

Manali–Kaza: shorter, but a steep 1-2 day altitude jump over Kunzum Pass — named as the route responsible for most AMS cases. Shimla–Kinnaur–Kaza: longer, gentler, the only route open in winter.

Altitude

3,650m to 4,650m+

Staged stops — Reckong Peo/Kalpa, then Tabo, before pushing to Kaza — are what actually prevents AMS, not just "drink water."

Connectivity

Kaza only, mostly

BSNL + Jio 4G both work in Kaza. A ~60km dead zone runs Gramphu → Batal → Chandratal → Kunzum Pass — the longest in Himachal.

Money & fuel

Kaza, or nowhere

The valley's only two ATMs sit in Kaza — both unreliable in peak season. Kaza also has the only petrol pump inside Spiti proper.

Best time

Depends what you want

Late May–mid-Oct for road access. Late Sept–early Oct for photography. Dec–March for snow leopards. Late Jan–mid-Feb for "whiteout" winter Spiti.

Where To Sleep, What To Eat
Homestays run the valley
Kaza is the only real hotel town.

Spiti Ecosphere network

A real community-tourism org (founded 2002) running a deliberately-capped homestay network across six villages — Langza, Komic, Demul, Lhalung, Dhankar, Mikkim. Up to three homestays per village, one guest room each. ₹700–1,800/person/night, usually meals included. Demul's rotational system means no traveler stays with the same family two years running.

Kaza & Tabo

Kaza is the only real hotel/guesthouse market — from ₹520 budget guesthouses to Hotel Deyzor (comfort tier, solar+generator power) and Zostel Spiti (backpacker dorms). Tabo has a genuine monastery guesthouse — stay with monks at nominal cost, a real option, not folklore.

On The Plate

Thukpa & Thentuk

Hand-pulled noodle soups — thukpa long-noodle, thentuk the thicker hand-torn version. The default homestay dinner.

Momos

Steamed or fried dumplings, everywhere — Kaza's Café Deyzor specifically called out for the best in town.

Chhurpi

Yak-milk cheese, tangy and firm — eaten standalone or worked into soups and stir-fries.

Tsampa

Roasted barley flour — the real dietary staple, predating the green-pea cash crop by generations.

Butter Tea (Po Cha)

Churned savory, not sweet — usually the first thing you're offered walking into any home.

Kaza's café scene (Sol Café, Yak Café, Café Deyzor) blends this with backpacker staples like pizza and pancakes; homestays are where the real version lives.

Field Notes
Words you'll actually hear in Spiti.
Pulled directly from the research, not a generic Tibetan-phrasebook list — every term here appeared in a real, sourced fact above.

ChortenA Buddhist stupa/shrine — Tabo alone has 23 of them.
AmchiA traditional Sowa Rigpa healer, pulse-diagnosis based. Just 8-10 remain practicing in Spiti today.

ChamThe ritual masked dance performed at monastery festivals — Key's Chaam, Kungri's devil-dance.
BuchenA lay Nyingma ritual performer — Mud village's Buchen lead Kungri's July sword-dance, not the monks.

PerakTurquoise-studded head ornament, married women only — historically a real marker of household status.

Beyond The Trail
Where a drive becomes an expedition.

Pin-Parvati Pass

5,300m+, 10-12 days, ~110km. "Very difficult" — glacier crossings, no technical gear needed but real route-finding risk. Connects lush Parvati Valley to Spiti's cold desert.

Parang La

5,538m+, ~8 days, 100km+. The primary real trekking connection between Spiti and Ladakh, ending at Tso Moriri. Remote, no rescue access en route.

Pin Valley National Park

1,825km², established 1987. Myth flagged — travel blogs quote "12-15 snow leopards" with no citation. Real science measures density, not a park headcount.

Chandratal Lake

~4,300m, "Moon Lake" — genuinely crescent-shaped. Camping on the shoreline is banned; designated zones sit 2-3km away.

After Dark
Sky Quality, tonight: reading…
What actually makes the sky different — and what's just a claim.

Spiti is not IDA/DarkSky International certified — that claim circulates on several travel sites and appears to be false. India's actual certified dark-sky sites are Hanle (Ladakh) and Pench Tiger Reserve (Maharashtra). No verified Bortle-scale measurement exists for Spiti at all — informal operator claims range from Class 1 to Class 2, with no cited instrument or survey behind either number.

What's real instead

The Spiti Dome Observatory opened in Kaza in July 2025 — Himachal Pradesh's first high-altitude public dome observatory, built under HIMCOSTE with two GOTO telescopes for public sessions.

Best window

July–September, aligned to new moon. Winter technically has clearer air, but the access routes are closed. Langza, Komic and Kibber are the most-cited viewpoints for their open, unobstructed horizons.

What The Guidebooks Miss
Genuinely offbeat, genuinely verified.

Lingti Frozen Waterfall

The one real, named waterfall inside Spiti proper — a roadside cascade ~60km from Kaza that freezes into ice formations in winter.

The Gue Mummy

A ~500-600-year-old naturally self-mummified monk, Sangha Tenzin, discovered after the 1975 earthquake — still displayed beside Gue Monastery.

Shilla Peak's 900m error

Surveyed in 1860 at 7,029m — briefly the presumed highest mountain ever climbed. Modern surveys corrected it to 6,132m.

Cold Desert Biosphere Reserve

September 2025 — Spiti became India's first Cold Desert Biosphere Reserve under UNESCO's programme, spanning 7,770km².

Stone medicine

Just 8-10 practicing Amchis remain. In Demul, they use a mineral called Chug Jhi — "male" and "female" stone, given cross-gender — for gastric ailments.

Correcting "Manikaran"

Multiple sites list Manikaran hot springs as "near Spiti" — it's actually a different valley entirely. No verified hot springs exist inside Spiti proper.
