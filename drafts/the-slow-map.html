A lake that forgot to become famous.

While Nainital became a ticket-booth, Naukuchiatal stayed asleep under its pine trees. Nine corners — and a local superstition: spot all nine in a single glance from one spot, and you earn moksha. Most tourists don't bother trying.

This is not a checklist destination. It rewards people who can sit still, walk slowly, and eat where the dog sleeps at the door.

§ 01 — The Soul of the Place

Who belongs here. And who, honestly, does not.

Every place has a temperament. Naukuchiatal is introverted. It doesn't dress up for you. Bring the right company — or leave it alone.

Come if you…

  • consider a lakeside bench + a book a full day's plan
  • like watching mist lift off water for 45 uninterrupted minutes
  • will choose a 2 km forest walk over a 12-stop itinerary
  • order filter coffee, not a frappé with whipped cream
  • travel with little ones who like boats, frogs, and pine cones
  • want your hotel to be quieter than your house

Stay away if you…

  • need a nightlife, a mall, or a “viewpoint selfie”
  • came to Uttarakhand for adventure sports adrenaline
  • want big crowds and a buzzing bazaar
  • expect Instagram-ready cafés every 200 metres
  • book without checking weekends (Sat–Sun gets Delhi traffic)
  • equate “more sightseeing” with a better holiday
§ 02 — When to Go (and When Not)

The lake has four personalities.

Each season rewrites the place. Pick carefully — the difference between going in May vs. October is the difference between a bus station and a monastery.

MAR · APR · MAY

Spring

Rhododendron in bloom, buransh juice at roadside stalls. March–mid-April is the sweet spot; from April 27 onward CBSE summer holidays run till June 10 — expect heavy Delhi-NCR traffic on weekends.

Go · Before Apr 27
JUN · JUL · AUG

Monsoon

Hill roads get dicey. But the forests turn electric green, cafés are empty, and you'll hear nothing but crickets at 3pm. Landslides are real.

Go · If brave
SEP · OCT · NOV

Autumn

The secret season. Post-rain clarity, 15–22°C days, Himalayas visible after monsoon washed the haze. October is the local's favourite.

Best
DEC · JAN · FEB

Winter

Cold (nights 2–5°C), rare snow, fireplaces lit everywhere. Christmas week gets booked, otherwise blissfully empty. Pack serious layers.

§ 04 — Where to Sleep

No chain hotels. Only character.

Ranked by soul, not by star rating. A 4-star with character beats a 5-star with a buffet, every time.

01
IHCL SeleQtions · heritage luxury

Naukuchia House, Naukuchiatal

The only full-service luxury stay on the lake. Palladian-inspired architecture, perched above the water, infinity pool, spa, heated pool, and an in-house kitchen that will serve Kumaoni dishes off-menu if you ask. Ranked on Booking.com, Tripadvisor, and MakeMyTrip; 4.1 stars from ~1,900 reviews.

Deluxe Garden View is the entry-level room. Ask specifically for a Lake View room — the upgrade is worth it at sunrise.
₹21,240per night (Deluxe, incl. taxes)

Resort · 38 rooms · 70 acres of oak forest

The Lake Resort, Naukuchiatal

Ranked #1 of 8 hotels in Naukuchiatal on Tripadvisor. Sprawls over 70 acres, houses Café Magic Forest on-site. Rooms plus Swiss tents — pick rooms for comfort, tents for the novelty. Adventure sports available.

Stay here if you want space to roam without leaving the property.
from ₹8,237per person

Boutique cottages · facing the lake

Cottages@Village

Cottages positioned directly opposite Naukuchiatal lake with Himalayan backdrop. Kayaking, paragliding, and mountain biking available on-site. Less polished than Naukuchia House, but the views from the terrace at dawn are the strongest on this list.

Best booked via Booking.com or MakeMyTrip — their direct rates tend to be higher than OTAs.

§ 05 — Walks & Hidden Trails

The walks the tourist taxis can't reach.

Kumaon's landscape is hiking country disguised as a tourist destination. The magic is in leaving the lake and climbing 200 metres up into the pine forest — you'll hear no one.

Distance · 2.0 km loop Elevation · +90 m Time · 1h 15m

The Eight Corners Trail

A marked loop through the oak forest above the lake's southwest shore. You see 8 of the 9 corners (the ninth is locals' folklore). Kid-safe. Ends near a spring tea-stall that serves ginger-honey tea.

Note: "Eight Corners" is the informal local name for the lake-rim trail — ask locals for the Jungliagaad / Naukuchiatal perimeter trail.

Kid friendly Marked trail Crowd-free
Distance · 3.5 km Elevation · +180 m Time · 2h

Jungalia Gaon · Forest Walk

Jangalia Gaon (also spelt Jungliagaon) sits 8 km from Naukuchiatal — an offbeat hamlet loved by birders and anglers. Climb through oak forest, pass fruit orchards; locals usually wave. Walk back or arrange a taxi pickup.

Kid friendly Barely anyone Villages
Distance · 4 km one-way Elevation · +260 m Time · 3h round

Hidimba Parvat via Bhimtal

Named for the Mahabharata's Hidimba. The last 800 m is steep — manageable for fit children with breaks. Summit has a tiny temple and genuinely nobody there on weekdays.

Moderate Empty weekdays Temple
Distance · 2.5 km Elevation · +50 m Time · 1h 30m

Bhalu Gaad Waterfall (Mukteshwar)

Park at Dhari village (10 km from Mukteshwar), then trek 2 km through dense jungle with three stream crossings and rock scrambles. ~45-60 min each way. Dip your feet in the cold pool at the base. Best March–June and September–November. Visit before 11 am.

Kid friendly Water Alt route
Distance · 6 km Elevation · +350 m Time · 4h

Sattal Birding Trail

Sattal hosts ~500 bird species, 525+ butterfly species. Several operators (Sattal Birding, Wild Kumaon) run guided walks with binoculars. April–May is breeding-plumage season. 45+ species on a good morning. Very gentle gradient.

Kid friendly Birds Reserve
Distance · 1.2 km Elevation · flat Time · 45m

Lands End (Bhimtal back road)

Skip the main Lands End viewpoint — it's crowded. Instead, walk the forest path behind Bhimtal's old PWD bungalow. Same view of the Gagas valley, half the people, and old pine that's 80+ years old.

Kid friendly Insider View
§ 06 — Slow Cafés & Book Corners

Places to sit still for three hours.

Not Instagram cafés. Real ones — where the owner knows the coffee bean, the book on the shelf, and the dog asleep in the corner. In that order.

Café Magic Forest

Naukuchiatal · at The Lake Resort

Wood-and-glass dining pavilion on the Lake Resort property. The café is reviewed as “better than the resort's own breakfast buffet” — go for the wood-oven pizzas in the afternoon. Instagram @cafemagicforest.

wood-fired pizza + filter coffee · afternoon light through the trees

iHeart Café Himalayas

Bhimtal · ~4.5 on Tripadvisor

Wooden-lodge exterior, stone-studded walls, beautiful graffiti and quaint wooden tables. Coffee + snacks + pasta + pizza + bakery. Some find the prices on the higher side — the ambience makes up for it.

cappuccino + a muffin · outdoor balcony for the hill view

Aipan Restaurant

Naukuchiatal · authentic Kumaoni

Named after traditional Kumaoni artwork. Owner-chef Gaurav Pant serves Kumaoni specialities — Bhatt ki Churkani, Nimbu Saan, Masala Papad, Aloo Gutke. Vegetarian. Located right at Naukuchiatal, near the lake market.

Bhatt ki Churkani with rice + a masala papad to start

The Kumaoni Farm Cafe

Dhulai village, Bhimtal · by reservation

A sincere revival of the lost cuisine of Kumaon — cooked traditionally on a wood-fired chulha, farm-fresh ingredients. Prior reservation only. Worth the phone call: reviews compare it to “food befitting a Michelin-star restaurant”.

the full Kumaoni thali · book 1–2 days ahead via Tripadvisor listing

The Birdsong Café

Mukteshwar · Simi Das's garden

Owned and run by Simi Das, surrounded by pear trees and seasonal flowers. Fantastic Kumaoni thali, plus cakes and coffee. One of the most praised cafés in Mukteshwar. (Note: often confused with Sattal — the well-known one is Mukteshwar.)

Kumaoni thali at lunch · filter coffee and home-baked cake after

Café De Mukteshwar

Mukteshwar · #2 of 17 on Tripadvisor

Rated 4.9/5. Locally-sourced everything, friendly reception, authentic Kumaoni and continental flavours. If the Himalayas are out, the window seat is unbeatable.

whatever's in-season · ask the staff what's been harvested that week

Chirping Tales Café

Mukteshwar · farm-to-table

The menu is built around fresh local ingredients, home-grown vegetables, local herbs, home-baked bread. Quieter than Café De Mukteshwar, gives you the slow-afternoon-read feeling. Official site: chirpingtales.com.

the day's bread + soup + Himalayan tea

Kilmora Shop & Café

Mukteshwar · Kumaun Grameen Udyog

A women's cooperative — “Kumaun Grameen Udyog” — selling hand-knitted textiles, apricot-skin products, organic herbs, homemade jams and juices. Small café attached. Buying here directly funds rural livelihoods.

apricot-kernel oil + a jar of kilmora-berry jam · your skin thanks you in hill air
§ 07 — Eat Like a Kumaoni

Six dishes that the menu won't tell you about.

Kumaoni food is rough, smoky, and deeply rooted in what the hill soil actually grows. Ask for these by name — they're not on printed menus, but every local kitchen makes them.

Bhatt ki Churkani
Black soybean curry · slow-cooked 3 hours

Thick, smoky, nothing like dal. The black soybean is soaked overnight and roasted before simmering. Eaten with steamed rice in winter — it's Kumaoni comfort food.

Where: Aipan Restaurant, Naukuchiatal
Aloo ke Gutke
Dry spiced potatoes · Jakhiya seed tempering

Potatoes roasted in mustard oil with a tiny wild seed called Jakhiya (only grows in Kumaon). Every house makes it differently. Gateway dish for kids who don't like "new food".

Where: any homestay — ask for it
Kafuli
Spinach & fenugreek curry · rice-flour thickened

An emerald-green curry so thick you could eat it with a spoon. Healthier than it tastes. Usually paired with madua (finger millet) roti instead of wheat — which is the point.

Where: The Kumaoni Farm Cafe, Bhimtal (by reservation)
Singori
Sweet · khoya wrapped in malu leaf

A conical sweet parcel wrapped in a fragrant wild leaf that flavours the khoya inside. Almost impossible to find outside Kumaon. Little ones love unwrapping it.

Where: sweet shops in Bhimtal Bazaar · ask for freshly-made
Bal Mithai
Chocolate-like roasted khoya fudge

Looks like chocolate truffles but is roasted khoya coated in sugar balls. Originated in Almora. Every Kumaoni child grew up on this. Good road-trip snack.

Where: any traditional Kumaoni sweet shop · Bhimtal, Bhowali or Almora
Buransh Sharbat
Rhododendron flower juice · April only

Made from the red rhododendron flowers that cover these forests in spring. Tart, rose-like, said to be good for the heart. Available roadside March–April.

Where: Kilmora Café, any roadside dhaba in spring

§ 09 — The Halo

Places near enough to reach before lunch.

47 km · 1h 30m drive

Mukteshwar

Pine ridge at ~2,286 m with open Himalayan views. Visit for the Bhalu Gaad waterfall trek (park at Dhari, 2 km each way, cross three streams), lunch at Café De Mukteshwar or Birdsong, and the 350-year-old Mukteshwar temple. Best on a clear morning — haze closes the view by noon.

36 km · 1h 15m drive

Ramgarh (Fruit Bowl of Kumaon)

Orchards of apples, apricots, peaches, plums, pears. Rabindranath Tagore wrote parts of Gitanjali here; the site is now known as Tagore Top. Some orchards offer “Orchard Discovery Walks” and fruit-picking experiences.

~50 km · 1h 30m drive

Peora village

Tiny Kumaoni hamlet at 6,600 ft (2,011 m), 10 km from Mukteshwar. A real offbeat destination — sparsely scattered on contoured valley slopes, pine forests, deep quiet. Go for the walk itself, not for “sights”.

40 km · 1h 20m drive

Kainchi Dham

Neem Karoli Baba's ashram. Darshan summer 6 AM – 7 PM, winter 7 AM – 6 PM. Closed Mondays except for morning & evening aarti. Go Tue–Sat morning before 9 am — weekdays outside mid-June are the calmest. June 15 Bhandara = chaos.

§ 10 — The Anti Bucket List

Things tourists do here. You shouldn't.

The whole point of coming to Naukuchiatal is to not behave like you're in Nainital. Here's what to skip — and what to do instead.

✗
Do not drive to Nainital for “The Mall Road”

It is a loud, congested bazaar with the same goods sold cheaper in Bhimtal. Instead: walk the Bhimtal back-road to the butterfly centre, or spend the time at iHeart Café.

✗
Do not book the Naukuchiatal boat ride package

The shared boat at the main ghat is crowded and loud. Instead: ask Naukuchia House (or any boutique hotel) to arrange a private shikara from their jetty. Costs ₹600–800 more, but it's just your family.

✗
Do not go to Sattal on a Sunday

Delhi day-trippers arrive by the busload. Instead: go Tuesday/Wednesday. Or stick to the Studio Café & birding reserve at the far end.

✗
Do not eat at “Pure Veg Family Restaurants” on the main road

Bland, tourist-priced, no Kumaoni food. Instead: eat at Kumaon Local Kitchen, or ask your homestay to cook. The cheapest meal will be the best meal.

✗
Do not visit during a long weekend

Delhi's upper-middle class treats Nainital-region as a weekend getaway. Check for Friday holidays + weekend combos. Instead: take a Wed–Sun trip. The difference in quiet is dramatic.

✗
Do not chase the “must-see” Himalayan viewpoint

There's no single spectacular viewpoint from Naukuchiatal (for that, drive to Mukteshwar). Instead: accept that this is a lake-and-forest trip, not a Himalaya-panorama trip. Different joy.

§ 11 — Speaking Kumaoni

Ten words that open doors.

Most locals speak Hindi, but swap in a Kumaoni word and watch the shopkeeper smile. Your daughter will love teaching them back in Delhi.

Jai Bhole
Default Kumaoni greeting. Works everywhere.
Tal
Lake. (Bhimtal = Bhim's lake.)
Gaon
Village. Jungliagaon = "wild village".
Pahadi
"Of the mountains" — used for food, people, fabric.
Harela
Kumaoni harvest festival · mid-July.
Phooldei
Spring flower festival · mid-March, children lead it.
Madua
Finger millet (ragi) — stronger, darker roti.
Buransh
Rhododendron flower · makes the April sharbat.
Bugyal
High-altitude meadow. A word Himachali folk envy.
Bhulo/Bhuli
Brother / Sister — for addressing a young local.
§ 12 — After Dark

The unadvertised sky.

Naukuchiatal has low light pollution — especially above the lake. On a clear new-moon night, you'll see the Milky Way without binoculars. Worth the late bedtime, whoever you're travelling with.

Best nights

New-moon nights from November to February. Clear winter air, no monsoon haze. Orion, Pleiades, Jupiter all visible. Check moon phase before booking.

Where to watch

Mukteshwar ridge (47 km drive) is darker than the valley — but Naukuchia House's upper terrace works too. Avoid the main Bhimtal market road (streetlights).

ARIES Observatory, Nainital

The Aryabhatta Research Institute (ARIES) at Manora Peak, Nainital, runs public stargazing evenings through a 14-inch telescope. Day visits: Mon / Wed / Fri 2:30 – 4 PM. Stargazing: typically 6:45 – 8 PM, book in advance. No entry fee. 9 km from Nainital town.

§ 15 — The Reading List

Books to carry. Or carry you there.

A slow trip deserves slow reading. These six are either set in Kumaon, written by people who lived here, or capture the hill-India sensibility. Start one before you leave Delhi.

The Man-Eaters of Kumaon
Jim Corbett · 1944
The definitive text. Corbett lived his whole life in these hills. Don't read at night — but do read the chapter on the Champawat tigress on the train in.
The Night Train at Deoli & Other Stories
Ruskin Bond · 1988
Short stories of hill-station India — Bond's cadence becomes the cadence of your trip. The title story is 12 pages and worth re-reading twice.
The Chipko Movement: A People's History
Shekhar Pathak · 2021
History of the forest-hugging protest that began 100 km from here. Explains the local relationship with the oak, the pine, and the outsider. Dense but transformative.
A Mountain in Tibet
Charles Allen · 1982
If Kumaon leaves you wanting more mountains — this traces the sources of four great rivers. The sensibility is identical to what you'll feel on Day 3.
The Room on the Roof
Ruskin Bond · 1956
For little ones to grow into. Bond wrote it when he was 17 — tell them that, and tell them the writer still lives in these hills.
The Himalayan Arc
Namita Gokhale (ed.) · 2018
An anthology — essays, poetry, memoir — from Pakistan to Myanmar along the Himalayan ridge. Read one essay per evening on your hotel balcony.
§ 16 — Practical & Emergency

The boring but vital page.

Nothing will go wrong. But if something does — especially with children — you want these on one screen. Screenshot this page before you leave.

Local hospital (minor)
Neelkanth Hospital, Naukuchiatal

Listed on Justdial. Handles everyday medical needs close to the lake — fevers, cuts, basic stabilisation.

Major hospital
B.D. Pandey Government Hospital

Nainital, ~35 km · ~1h drive. Nainital's principal government hospital for trauma and serious care. For anything serious, head here first.

Emergency number
112 — India's unified emergency

Works on all networks (Jio / Airtel / BSNL). Women's helpline 1091. Child helpline 1098. Tourist police Uttarakhand 1364.

Pharmacy
Bhimtal Main Market

Multiple chemists along the Bhimtal bazaar stretch. Most open 9 AM – 9 PM. For child formulations, ask at Naukuchia House reception — they'll direct you to the closest one they use.

Mobile signal
Jio & Airtel

Reliable around the lake and Bhimtal town. Signal thins in Jangalia Gaon and Peora. A pre-paid data plan with one of these will cover 95% of the trip.

ATM + cash
Bhimtal Bazaar

SBI, Bank of Baroda, and PNB ATMs on the main road. Carry ₹8,000–10,000 cash — some village tea-stalls, homestays, and taxi drivers prefer cash over UPI.

Verified taxi services
Gurukripa · MS Taxi · Kathgodam Taxi Service

Gurukripa: +91-7500047111 (9 AM – 7 PM) · MS Taxi Kathgodam: +91-9719336728 · Kathgodam Taxi Service: +91-7351721351. Kathgodam → Naukuchiatal ≈ ₹1,200. Book 2 days ahead in peak season.

Altitude / health
No altitude issues

At 1,220 m, AMS isn't a concern. Drink more water than in Delhi — the dry hill air dehydrates faster. For children, keep ORS sachets handy.
